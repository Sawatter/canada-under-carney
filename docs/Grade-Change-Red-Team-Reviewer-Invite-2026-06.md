# Grade-Change Red-Team Reviewer Invite - June 2026

- **Purpose:** Copy-ready language for recruiting one reviewer with a different
  political prior than the editor, to run the grade-change red-team check.
- **Status:** Ready to use.
- **Protocol:** [Grade-Change-Red-Team-Protocol.md](Grade-Change-Red-Team-Protocol.md)
- **Fires on:** a published grade change. The reviewer is on-call for those, not for
  every cycle.

---

## Short Invite

I run a public scorecard that grades the federal Carney government, and I'm looking
for one person whose politics are different from mine to sanity-check my grade
changes.

Here's the ask. When a grade on the board moves, I'd send you a short redacted pack
for that one dimension: the evidence links, the band rules, the modifier rules, and
the criterion the grade is supposed to meet. It leaves out my own write-up and the
current published grade. You'd independently pick the band the evidence supports and
answer one question: would this same evidence get this same grade under a different
governing party? If your answer is no, you tell me where it reads as slanted.

It should take about 30-45 minutes per grade change, and it would not be every week.
The reason I want someone who does not share my leanings is that the whole point of
the check is to see whether the grade holds up to a reader who is not inclined to
give this government the benefit of the doubt, or inclined to be harder on it than
the evidence warrants. I'm asking you to apply the rule, not to agree with me.

Protocol: `docs/Grade-Change-Red-Team-Protocol.md`

## Longer Invite

A fair criticism of any scorecard is that even a published rubric can hide the
grader's own politics. I already run a party-symmetry test on every grade move (would
the same evidence produce the same grade under a different party in power?), but right
now I'm the one applying it, and I set the grade in the first place. That's the gap I
want your help closing.

What I'm after is one reviewer whose political priors run different from mine, to act
as a red team on grade changes. You would not need to agree that the project should
exist or that any particular grade is right. The job is the opposite of a loyalty
test: I want you to push on whether the grade follows from the rule, from a starting
point that is not mine.

When a grade changes, I'd send you, for that dimension only:

- the evidence links behind the change
- the band criteria and the plus/minus rules
- the modifier rules
- the specific criterion the grade is meant to meet

I'd leave out:

- the current published grade
- my rationale and judgment notes
- the critics / defenders prose
- the changelog write-up for the move

What I'd want back is short:

- the band you independently place it in, citing the threshold text
- plus / flat / minus, or whole-letter-only where that applies
- your read on the party-symmetry question: same evidence, different governing
  party, same grade? yes / no / explain
- anywhere the grade reads as politically driven rather than evidence driven
- your confidence, and anything you needed but I didn't give you

If you land a full letter away from where I did, or you can't answer the symmetry
question with a clean yes, that's the signal I'm looking for. I'd have to address it
in writing before the grade publishes, or hold the move. To keep the record honest,
I'd note your self-described leaning in general terms (so the difference in priors is
real and on the record), and your check flags the grade. It does not set it. I still
own the decision and have to defend it.

This is separate from the other outside-reader test I run (the inter-rater pilot),
which deliberately screens politics out because it measures whether the rubric is
reproducible. This one deliberately screens politics in.

---

## How to use this

1. Recruit one reviewer whose self-identified leaning differs from the editor's.
   Record the leaning in general terms.
2. On the next grade change, assemble the redacted per-dimension pack described above
   (mirror the redaction list in the inter-rater packet).
3. Send the pack. Collect the short worksheet back before publishing the move.
4. If the reviewer diverges by a full letter or cannot give a clean symmetry yes,
   resolve it in `judgmentDetail` or hold the move.
5. Once the first real run is done, update the Methodology tab's limits block to say
   the red-team check is active, with what it found.
